# NOIR CITY — things to do (the design; lanes O5 and O7 build them, O2 and O4 own their contests)

No missions, no list. Each of these is **found by looking**, started by
walking up to it, played by the rulebook's verbs, won or lost by the
standard's rules (can I lose, can I win, do I know how), and answered by
the city (the heat, the crowd, the news). Each pays `$` or costs it, and
each leaves a mark a player can see later. Nothing is required: a
player who ignores all of it still has the whole city.

The rule of the middle holds: one objective line while a thing is taken
up (`THE RACE: 2nd.`, `FARE: 48th St.`), one card at the first need,
the nudge only when stuck, the fail's black with the fix in six words.
The minimap marks the thing taken up and nothing else.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
## 1. The gym's bouts (the Tenements; O4's fight, O5's room)
**Found**: the gym's lit window on Hester St, the sign, the sound of the
bag and the bell through the door; inside, the ring under its lamp, men
on the benches, the trainer at the table (E: `Fight? $10 on it.`).
**Played**: three boxers of a level (the kid, the journeyman, the
champ), each a kind with his own tell and his own fault; the bout is
the crowd brawl's one-on-one in a ring (the ropes stop me, a corner is
a trap, the crowd's noise rises with the exchanges); three rounds of
sixty seconds with a bell; a knockdown counts (his and mine: ten
seconds on the floor and up, or out). The purse: $20, $50, $150; a bet
on myself doubles it. Losing: the hospital is not it: I wake on the
bench, lighter by the bet, and the trainer says one line.
**The city answers**: a champ beaten is known: the newspaper's line,
the gym's men nod, the gang of the Tenements leaves me be for a night.
**Legible**: the first bout teaches nothing new (the fight's cards were
taught on the street or come here if not).

## 2. The race (the Yards; O2's cars)
**Found**: at night, three cars idling at the Straight's south end
under the gasworks' lamps, a man with a flag, a crowd on the fence
(the sound of engines from a block away); E on the flagman: `$50 in.
Three laps.` A player with no $50 is told so and may watch (the cars
go; the race runs without him).
**Played**: three laps of a course on the Yards' roads (the Straight,
the turn by the rail gate, the loop by the sheds: about 1.4 km a lap),
against three drivers of three tempers (the clean one, the shunter, the
fast one who crashes once in three) on O2's pilot with a racing line;
my car is the car I came in (a lorry races badly: that is a choice); the
course is marked by lamps on oil drums and the crowd at the corners;
the objective line counts the lap and the place. Winning: the pot
($200). Losing: nothing but the $50. A crash that wrecks me: `Out.`,
and the race goes on without me. Cheating (a shortcut through a yard) is
allowed and the shunter does it too.
**The city answers**: the police at heat: a patrol car that sees the
race raises 1 for everyone and chases; the race goes on with the cops in
it. A wreck on the course stays for the next lap.

## 3. Fares (Downtown and the Tenements; O2's taxi)
**Found**: get into a taxi (a hijacked one, or the garage's); a hand
goes up on the kerb within sight (the kit's figure, a bag, a hat); E at
the kerb by him: he gets in and says the place (`The Palace Hotel.`,
`Pier 7.`, `The gym, and step on it.`): the objective line, the
minimap's mark, the meter in the HUD's corner counting up ($2 a block,
a tip for speed under a minute per kilometre, nothing for a crash).
**Played**: drive there through the traffic; he comments at a crash
(`Easy!`), at a shot (`Let me out!`: he goes, no fare), at the police
(`Not my problem, pal.`); arriving: E at the kerb, the fare and the
tip, he goes in. Five fares a night find me; a fare taken while the
heat is up is a passenger in the chase (he screams; the cops see a
taxi, not me, for the first ten seconds: a way to lose them).
**The city answers**: a fare dropped in the wrong place walks off
unpaid; a fare driven into the river is heat 3.

## 4. Hideouts (the Docks and the Tenements; O4's guns, O5's rooms)
**Found**: a warehouse on the quay with two men at its door and a
lamp over it, a tenement's cellar bar with a man on the stoop; the
gang's colour on the door. Walking up is allowed (they watch; one
asks); drawing is the start.
**Played**: a shootout through the place (the warehouse's aisles and
stacks, the cellar's bar and back room): six to eight men with cover
and flanks, the kit's tells; the safe in the back (E: it opens, $300
and a box of rounds) or the boss at the desk (a man with a Tommy gun
and a tell of his own). Leaving: out the way I came or the back way
(a fork). The place stays cleared for the night (the bodies gone by
morning, the men back in two nights with a grudge: they watch me on
the street).
**The city answers**: heat 2 at the first shot if a witness is near
(the docks at night: few), the gang's own men come from the street
while it goes on (the fight spills out), the newspaper's line.

## 5. The bounty board (the police station; O5's room, O3's people)
**Found**: a board in the station's hall with three faces a night, a
name, a street, `$100`. Not a mission: three men who are somewhere in
the city tonight, each a kind with a habit (one drinks at the Docks'
bar, one drives a hearse, one stands on his corner with his gang).
**Played**: find him (he is where his habit puts him), take him in
(fists until he is down, then E: `Come on.`: he walks ahead of me to
the station, tries to run once; or my car with him in the back), or
kill him (the money halves). The station's desk pays.
**The city answers**: a man taken in is gone from the street; his gang
has a grudge; the newspaper.

## 6. The el (O1's track, O7's ride)
**Found**: the station's stair, the platform, the train that waits.
**Played**: get on (E at the open door), ride a loop (five minutes of
the city from above, the rain on the windows, the lamps going by; a
still or two of the view if the look is good), get off at any station.
Not a contest: a view. A player chased onto the el loses the police
at the next station if they did not see him board.

## 7. The theatre (Downtown; O5's lobby, O7's reel)
**Found**: the marquee's bulbs, the lobby's lamp, the man in the booth
(E: `$1.`); the house: rows of hats in the dark, the screen.
**Played**: sit (E on a seat), a short reel on the screen (ninety
seconds of the ink's own stills with cards, a newsreel of the city: the
night's headlines as moving panels), leave any time. A player with
heat can wait out a search here (the cops do not come in).

## 8. The phone box (every district; O2's garage)
**Found**: a call box (O1's) with E: `Call the garage.` A car of the
garage's (one I stored) is brought to the kerb by a driver in two
minutes for $20; he walks off. The first way a player gets his good
car back after the police took it.

## 9. The matchbooks (every bar; O5's rooms, O7's pause card)
Each bar has its matchbook on its counter (E: taken); the pause card
shows the row (eight bars, their names in their own letterings, the
blanks unlit). Nothing is unlocked by them: a collection, the city's
names, a reason to go into every bar.

## 10. The newspaper (O1b's stands, O3's news)
Every stand has today's paper; E reads the headline, which is the
city's own news of what I did last: `GUNFIGHT ON GRAND AVE: THREE
DEAD`, `TAXI STOLEN UNDER COP'S NOSE`, `CHAMP BEATEN AT HESTER ST GYM`,
`RACE AT THE YARDS: POLICE GIVE CHASE`. The line is picked from the
police system's and the activities' events by weight (the worst thing
wins; nothing happened: `RAIN TO CONTINUE`). The theatre's reel reads
the same lines. This is the cheapest way the city shows it remembers.

## The seams
- `things.start(id, opts)` / `things.end(id, result)` (O7): the objective
  line, the minimap's mark, the money, the news line, the save's record
  of what was done tonight (so a cleared hideout stays cleared).
- `news.say(line, weight)` (O3 emits for crimes; the activities emit
  their own).
- `money.add(n)` / `money.take(n) -> bool` (O4 began `save.money`).
- Each activity's contest proves `idle / bad / medium / good` like a
  chapter's beat did; its lane names the levers in its report.
