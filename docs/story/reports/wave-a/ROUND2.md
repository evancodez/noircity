## rootCauses

Spinning torso (it falls in part to owner 1, but it was visible in my scene too: Dot's chest wound round during her pour, and any glancing patron can do it). Hood.postPose turns the spine, chest and head toward a look-at target by multiplying their quaternions. three's AnimationMixer only writes a bone when its mixed value differs from last step's (PropertyMixer.apply compares its two accumulation buffers). A clip that holds the spine still, such as a static standing pose or the held last frame of a one-shot like rise, br_guard or ko, is written once and then never again. So the look-at's turn is added on top of itself every step, and the chest winds through full turns. Probe: Dot's chest heading went from (0.13,-0.99) to (-0.54,-0.84) to (-0.8,0.59) in 1 s with no clip change. Fix: src/cast/diner/steady.js marks each figure's mixer bindings as stale before every pose (sets last step's buffer to NaN, via the Hood's own `script` hook that runs before the mixer). The mixer then writes every bone every step, so the look-at and the IK are always laid on the clip's pose. After the fix, the chest is constant (0.99,-0.11) through the pour. dinerRoom installs it on every diner figure. Owner 1 can call steady(fig) on Mack in the fight: it is very likely the same cause as his 360s on the rise into guard and on the ground. src/enemies was not touched. Wrong pour angle: the old pour posed Dot's arm (handTR, handR -80 degrees) and hung the bottle from her hand bone with a fixed 'body' grip, so the bottle's angle was whatever the wrist gave, and the stream was a Bezier aimed from the bottle's top at the glass. Now the bottle's path and tilt are authored about the counter's axis, the arm is solved onto it, and the stream is a parabola from the lip. Picking up and putting down the glass looked wrong because the hand was keyed and the glass rode a socket that was not under the fingers: fingers went over the rim, and the glass swung about the wrist between keys. Now the glass's own path is authored and the hand is solved onto a grip fitted on the rig. Floating shards and the line between thumb and index are owner 1's and owner 3's.

## rootCauses

Stick or line between thumb and index: HandRig.buildHandGeometry built the web as a separate thin ellipsoid flap from the thumb's MCP to the index MCP, skinned half to the thumb. When the thumb opened, the flap stretched into a thin rod standing across the gap, and the ink traced its outline and its id and normal edges. Removed for the bare hand. The web is now the palm's own radial edge, skinned in part to the thumb, so one surface folds and there is no seam for the ink to find. The glove keeps its flap.
Characters as balloon blobs: two causes. (a) The print: cast figures went through the Hood figure path (hoodF), whose soft knee still let a pendant-lit figure print its smooth shading as greys, so every rounded form read as a lit balloon. Fixed with a cast print class: a flat dark mass, light shapes by albedo printed flat with torn edges, and a rim. (b) The shape: domed sleeve heads, mitten or ellipsoid hands, sphere-like fists and domed caps. Partly fixed (see 3.1 Shape). The torso and arm tubes remain smooth.
Speckled knuckles: the ink's skin highlight (a pow-60 spec lobe) printed paper on the rig's small knuckle spheres. Turned off.
Hard wrist line in the fight: the round bare-forearm lathe met the flat hand and its wrist ellipsoid at an angle. The forearm is now elliptical at the wrist and turned with the hand.
Room too dark and figures against black: nothing lit what stood behind the people, and the window patches were so weak (0.0095) that the ink's crush ate them. Fixed with painted light on the end wall, the floor and the windows (3.3).
Pour bottle as a flat orange cylinder: the liquid mesh took the amber class, which printed one flat honey value up to the silhouette; the label was the dim atlas and faced away. Fixed with an in-vessel amber class that darkens edge-on, plus a light label front and back. Its size in Dot's hand is owner 2's.
Weak counter shadows: the pendant hung straight over the objects, so their shadows fell under them. Now it hangs back and is aimed forward.
The torso 360s, the pour angle and the floating shards belong to owners 1 and 2 and were not touched.
