   times in a step, it lets me go; hands off twelve seconds, the fight is on;

4. **The fight**: every click is a jab at once (60 to 280 ms, either button,
   from anything I am doing but reeling from his blow); held on past the jab
   the fist comes back cocked and loads - three ticks - and lands big. Every
   blow of his that must be dodged, and everything he throws, gets the cue:
   a third of a second of real time, the world at a third, my hands and look
   at full speed; toward the light, a slip and he is open; toward the dark, or
   late, it lands. His jab and his answer are told by his body alone. Low on
   health, `One more and you're down.`; down, the black says why (`Too slow.`,
   `Wrong way.`, `Caught swinging.`, `Into his guard.`, `Down.`), up again in
   about ten seconds. The street door, tried, rattles on its bar: `Barred.`.
5. **The finish** as before, out through the nearer window.
6. **After**: `See to him.` Two ways out: E lifts the bar (the hand closes on
   it and swings it up onto its keeper) and the door gives as I walk into it;
   or SPACE over the sill of the bay he went through, down onto the pavement
   beside him. E search; the matchbook; folded in it **Ames's card**, turning
   over in my fingers - J. AMES, PRIVATE INVESTIGATIONS, HARBOR BLDG. RM 304,
   crossed through in the crew's red, every word reading through the wax. E;

## The cue: three designs, one kept

Drawn and compared on the same moment (his right hook at its commit, 1440 x
900; `02_cue_designs`): **ink** (kept) - the dark side a loaded brush laid on
from its edge, torn inner edge, bristles; the light side paper torn open, white
bristles, three brush strokes sliding toward it; the fist marked by three white
strokes trailing it from the dark side. **wash** (`?cue=wash`) - a soft ink
wash over the dark half and one big stroke to the light: in a dark room the
wash barely shows, and the stroke reads as a UI bar. **lines** (`?cue=lines`) -
speed lines from the dark edge converging on the fist: says where it comes
from, not where to go. Only the ink one says "go that way" without being told.

## The user's old boxing game (shittyboxing)

- A tap under 250 ms was a jab, 250 .. 500 a light haymaker, past 500 a heavy -
  all **on release**: the very thing he now felt as "nothing comes out until I
  let go"; here the jab is on the press and the load comes after it.
- His tell was a **red flash on the side the fist comes from, dodge the other
  way**, the wind-up lasting half again so it could be read: the cue here is
  that idea in ink (the dark side, the light side), with the slowdown.
- A clean dodge was rewarded (the KO meter, a brief slow-motion opening) and
  spamming was taxed (stamina); here a slip opens him for a counter, and
  punching into his guard is answered.

## Left (most felt first)

1. **The cue's window and speed were tuned against models of players, not a
   person's hands.** 0.34 s of real time at a third of the world's speed: the
   real-input bots (their reaction plus the DevTools round trip) and the
   in-page styles say a practised reader answers nearly all and a struggling
   first-timer about half; nobody has felt it at full speed in a real browser
   with a real mouse. It is two numbers (`CUE.window`, `CUE.k`).
2. **A good player wins quickly**: the taught bot won its two fights in 55 and
   65 s each, the lesson included (one to two minutes for both; the brief
   says "two to three minutes"). The cue makes every big blow of his an
   opening for a player who reads it; his hit points (116) and the manner hold
   were left as version 4 had them, because the user liked the fight's length
   and the struggling first-timer already takes two to four minutes. If the
   fight should be longer for a good player, his hit points are the lever.
3. **His fist is marked by three trailing strokes, not rimmed** (the brief's
   "rimmed in white"): a rim round a moving fist read as a reticle in the
   first try; the strokes read as a comic's fist on its way. A real rim (an
   inverted hull on the hand, lit) is not built.
4. **The room clearing passes close**: the couple squeeze past Mack with 0.19
   m between bodies, slower and with their shoulders turned (a spine and chest
   twist laid on the run cycle, no clip of its own), and they are still in
   the room for the lesson's first card or two. Nobody passes through anybody.
5. **The door has no hand on it**: pushed by walking into it, pulled or pushed
   with E, it moves, but no hand of mine is on the push bar or the handle. The
   bar is lifted by the watcher's car-door grip (`carDoor`) kept on the bar
   as it swings up. Pulled from the street with me standing in its swing, it
   stops at me and I must step back (held open while I do).
6. **The check under the pendant**: its face is unlit (a lit page printed
   white, print and all); DINER and the red number read, her pencil only
   faintly at a glance. Chapter III shows it close and large (lane C5).
7. **The held first cue** (the lesson's first hook): the world is held by the
   kit's crawl and the cue's own third; the hook reaches me after about three
   seconds of real time if no key is pressed, before the kit's own six. Seen
   in shot mode and by the bots only.
8. **The music is not ducked under the cue** (the rain and the room's bed are).
9. **Pier 9** is lane C7's to rebuild: its fight passes by real input on these
   rules (`pier_fight.json`), its first cue teaches the dodge for a player who
   began there.
10. The fight's black (1.6 s) and its word are the brawl's own, drawn with the
    kit's lettering; it is not `game.fail` (FIGHT.md §8: losing a fist fight
    is not failing the chapter).
