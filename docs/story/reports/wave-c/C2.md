## The second pass: what changed, as a player would feel it

**Played cold first** (the Browser pane at a quarter speed, then headless
plays): the cards came without their keys (the shim's text), the kit's low
line said "Get to cover" to a man already down behind the desk, the rise
card came before I was behind the desk, the rooms were mid-grey and the
frosted glass dark, the street a flat slab under a grey sky. Under the hood:
after the first beat the men's grid had every doorway shut (a shut leaf
shrunk to a point still blocked the doorway's middle), so men in later beats
could not come through doors at all - the old proofs passed against men who
could not reach me.

- **Lane C1's kit in, the shim gone**: the beats declare their objective
  lines; the cards are C1's with the key drawn (`[C] get low`, `[RMB] rise
  and aim`, `[R] reload`, `K.teach(id)`; the crouch once more in a beat
  failed twice; a beat played cold teaches what it needs); the fall is
  `game.fail` (the kit's words, `K.why()`); the ready beat's rise card only
  once I am behind the desk.
- **The warning reads the moment** (`K.warning()`, every step): `Stop him!`
  (the heavy close), `Your cover won't hold. Move!`, `They have your angle.
  Move!` (down, a man has me - whatever my health), `One's going round on
  your left/right.` (a flanker on his way), `Get down!` (hurt, standing in a
  line), `Stay down. Let it mend.` (hurt, down, nobody on me). Held a second,
  never flickering. And a line that changes its words is one warning: the
  kit's "a second before every fall" is no longer given again by each
  change of words (`[C2]` in C1's `ui/kit.js`, one line: a bad player stood
  at 1 hp through a street of such changes).
- **Men through doors as men do**: the grid has every door as it stands
  open (the leaf a thing to go round), the dead out of it, locked doors
  shut; a man whose way crosses a shut door reaches for it, it swings, its
  sound, through (a leaf that would swing into him: a step back first); a
  locked one rattles and holds; an entry that unlocks a door opens the grid
  through it; a way that will not open in 12 s is given up (`guns_doors`).
- **Standing up in the open is the losing way, cover the winning one**:
  the fire tokens are room for the player who fights from cover - a man
  standing up draws every gun; a man standing still in a gunman's line is an
  easier mark (his settle x0.6); a gunman whose mark stands up, with nothing
  coming near him, keeps firing (two more bursts) instead of going back. The
  pistol's round stays the brief's 17 (tried 24 and 20: still in the open
  against two, down in 2.6 s - under the brief's three to four - and the
  good and clumsy hands suffered).
- **In time to turn**: a man who has me from off my screen (a flank, a door
  at my back) needs 0.45 s more before his settle, and the warning says
  `Behind you!`. Nobody waits the clock out: nine seconds without a line on
  me, a man comes straight in (a pin once stood still for 150 s).
- **The Tommy gunner pins, he does not delete**: wider, climbing faster (a
  flank by him is not one burst and down). The heavy: 250 hp (five revolver
  rounds), a hit stops him 0.45 s, shorter bursts further apart. The pin's
  line says the moment: `Down through the burst. Up when he reloads.`
- **The lean** works where it should (round a jamb or anything over a
  standing eye, on the view's own line); under ~1.4 m is risen over.
- **Heard**: the crouch down and up, the rise over the cover.
- **The look**: the rooms darker (pools are the light), the corridor lit so
  its doorways frame the men as shapes, a pool inside room A's door (a man
  coming through steps into the light), a pool on the lobby's reception
  counter, the frosted glass lit from behind
  by the nearest lamp on the far side (the men are shadows on it), the
  warehouse lower with the city's towers over its parapet and a lamp over
  each opening (pools and rain in their cones).
- **The proofs widened**: `guns_clumsy` (WAVE_C §7's first-timer, three
  tries a contest), `guns_doors`, `guns_walk` (the jank walk and the lean);
  every try's record now says how fast a fall came and where the rounds
  into me came from (pose, kind, flank, through cover); the good hand shoots
  shadows on frosted glass, moves on the warning and off a spent spot,
  watches a man going round, ducks when hit.

### 1. BLOCKER - standing up and trading won the pin and the door

What made it win, read from the recorded brains (`c2fix/open_door_rec*`):
a hit sent every man back behind his cover with his aim reset (and the
round's "near miss" took 60 % of his settle again), so a man stepping out
into a pre-aimed doorway never fired; 30 % of torso hits also staggered him
for a second (`Game.damageHood`'s old random stagger); the two door men came
out one at a time; a man stepping out walked 1.2 s with his gun up and
settled before firing; half their rounds at the jamb went into the wall
beside them (the gun hand behind the edge); and the kit's mercy (no fall
before a warning has been up a second) kept a stand-up trader alive at 1 hp,
because "Get down!" only came once he was under 45. What was done:

- **A hit, by where I shoot from** (`gunman.js onHit`): every hit is a
  flinch (the body jerks a step, `Hood.flinch`; half his settle and 0.18 s
  before it grows again; a man already firing goes on, his next rounds
  wider for 0.35 s; a Tommy gunner's burst climbs as if eight rounds
  longer). Shot by a man fighting **from cover** (down, or risen over it) he
  is the one out-shot: he ducks back behind his own, his aim gone (as the
  first owner had it for every hit). Shot by a man **standing in the open**
  he stays and trades. (A hit that never sent him back made the clumsy
  first-timer, who fights from cover, lose two contests three times each.)
  No random stagger for a kit pistol man or Tommy gunner (the heavy keeps
  his; `Game.js`, `[C2]`, the rng drawn as before). The man hit is no
  longer also "shot past" (`Guns.playerRound`).
- **The settle is earned each time out**: without a line it drains whatever
  he is doing (it was kept behind cover: out again, he fired with none).
- **A doorway worked by two**: the first at the jamb waits (up to 4 s) for
  his mate on his way, then they go out together; a man going to work a

- **A man standing in a gunman's line is his game**: no pause between his

## C2b: the dead block nobody, and no man spins (after the user played the test bed)

### 1. The dead block nobody

**Why the men got stuck.** A body became a circle in the stage's
colliders 1.35 s after the fall. The player was meant to walk round it.
The living were meant to ignore it: `Guns.resolveMan` set every corpse
circle's radius to 0 while it resolved a man. But `Colliders.resolve`
pushes a circle out to `c.r + r`, so a zero-radius corpse still pushed
every living man 0.32 m off the body's centre. Every body was an
invisible post that the living were shoved off and stuck on. So the
user's "enemies get stuck on them" was a bug, not a design. "You can't
walk over them" was the design, and he asked for it gone.

**Now.**
- The dead are in no colliders at all. `K.corpses` is a list of records
  `{ x, z, r, hood, ax, az, bx, bz }` (torso, head, feet), read from the
  bones each step.
- A body still lies where it fell: down the wall, over the cover, in the
  doorway.
- Rounds stop in it or not as before. They use the Hood's hit volumes,
  never the circle.
- The men's nav grid (`Covers.rebuild`), `Bodies.steer` and the doors'
  leaves never read the dead.
- **The step**: in the gun mode the eye goes up `GUN_MODE.overBody`
  (6.5 cm) times `K.overBody(x, z)`. That value is 0..1 along the body's
  length, head to feet: full within 0.2 m of the line, nothing past 0.5 m.
  It eases in at 14/s and out at 9/s, with a soft cloth sound as the foot
  goes on.
- Files: `src/guns/Guns.js` (`resolveMan`, `overBody`, `stepBodies`),
  `src/player/modes/gun.js`, `src/guns/Bodies.js` (comment).

### 2. No man spins

**Why.** The only writer of a living man's yaw is `HoodAI.faceTo` (the
short way, at a capped rate). So the spins were short-way turns that kept
going the same way. The counter (below), run on the integration code,
caught turns of **300 - 389 degrees within 2.5 s**. The worst one-second
total was 289 degrees, so the turns were not fast; they just went on too
far. The worst cases were in `back`, `aim` and `move`. The causes:
- A man turned away (~180 degrees) to walk a step back to his cover or out
  to his spot, then turned to face me again. From facing away, either way
  is the short way, and he often went on round: a full circle in two
  seconds.
- At a path's end, at full pace, his turning circle (0.4 m) was wider than
  the 0.3 m he had to come within, so he could loop round the point.
- His look-at twisted the chest 0.8 rad one way and then 0.8 rad the other
  in one frame as the target crossed his back.
- Aiming, he turned at 7 rad/s (400 degrees a second).

**Now**, for the kit's men only (`h.turnSafe`, set by `K.spawn`):
- `faceTo` keeps a decaying sum of how he has been turning (~1.5 s), and a
  turn of more than ~140 degrees goes back the way he came (it unwinds),
  keeping that way to the end.
- Near a path's end and off his line, he slows to 0.3 of his pace.
- His look fades out for a target behind him (past ~105 degrees).
- Aiming and firing he turns at 5.5 rad/s (~315 degrees a second).

The wave game's and the battle's men are untouched. A first version
changed every Hood, and the golden battle frames (`battle_t15/30/45`,
`end`: autopilot simulations) diverged.

Files: `src/enemies/HoodAI.js` (`faceTo`, `follow`), `src/enemies/Hood.js`
(the look), `src/enemies/brains/gunman.js` (aim rate), `src/guns/Guns.js`
(`turnSafe`).

**The counter**: `Guns.stepSpin` gives `K.spin`
(`{ sec, rev, turn, who, whoTurn }`). `sec` is the worst second's sum of
|dyaw|, `rev` the worst one-way sweep within a second, `turn` the worst
within 2.5 s. A sweep over 300 degrees is also logged as an event `spin`.
