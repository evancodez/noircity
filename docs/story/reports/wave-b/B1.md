## What plays, beat by beat

Every beat has a `setup`, so `?scene=body&beat=<beat>` renders it. Saves happen at `body`, `landing` and `note`.
Shot-mode strips can take `act=<name>@<t>,...` (kneel, wounds, fist, marks, taut1, taut2, pull, climb, take, pocket, join).
Each act does at scene time t what the player's key would do.

1. **approach**. I stand on the pavement by Mack, out cold in the glass. The B4 or B2 window is out, per the save. The view looks down the pavement to the alley's mouth.
   - At 1.5 s a black sedan rolls out of the alley on its own rail. It turns left off the kerb and opens up down the avenue. Its tail lamps are the crew's red, and `carAway` plays (engine, gears, tyre hiss).
   - The el train is cued over the avenue at 2.9 s.
   - Ames's grey felt hat turns in the kerb gutter, tipped on its brim, and drifts with the run of the water. Ripples come off it.
   - The alley's wall lamp is lit. Its one cone falls in the rain over the doorway, and it is the only goal in the frame.
   - Walking within 3.6 m of the body is the next beat.
2. **body**. Ames (cast kind `victim`) lies on his back under the lamp. His head is to the wall and turned away. Blood runs from under him in a narrowing thread down the fall of the flags to the drain.
   - He has one shoe off (a sock; the shoe lies by the drain), cracked round spectacles by his head, and paper crumpled in his right fist. The two wounds are soaked red on his shirt front.
   - **E kneel**: the eye eases down beside him in 0.9 s, with no cut. The mode is `seated` on a kneel anchor, eye 1.02 m.
   - I then E what I look at, one prompt at a time:
     - **the wounds**: the eye goes to his chest. "Two. In the chest. He was standing." A dashed chalk outline of a standing man in a hat rises where he stood, with the two wounds ringed, and the eye goes to it.
     - **his hand**: my right hand comes in over his fist. The fingers work into it and the wet paper comes away into my fingers. It comes up before my eyes and goes into my coat. The eye leans in and comes back. This takes 1.95 s.
     - **the marks**: the eye goes to the two bullet holes in the brick above where he stood, then along to the outline.
   - Once all three are seen, the strings begin.
3. **strings** (puzzle A). The eye glides 1.3 s out to the alley's mouth. The lens goes to 60 degrees and the plane of the shots is seen face on: the south wall, its two pins, the outline, and the fire escape above across the alley.
   - One prompt: `MOUSE  the string through the wound`. The mouse carries the string's free end one to one on that plane. The HUD's ink cross is at my end.
   - A white string runs from the pin to my end. A faint ray runs on from my end to whatever it strikes (a raycast into the alley's meshes).
   - Through the wound (3.4 degrees or 7 cm) it goes taut, with a pluck (`stringTaut`) and a twitch that dies in 0.35 s. It stays. The pointer jumps to the second pin.
   - The second string crosses the first at the landing. On the solve, lightning whitens the frame, the red half on the landing's edge glints for 2.2 s, and the view pushes slowly toward the crossing.
   - The hint comes after 40 s without progress, three levels.
4. **landing**. I walk. At the fire escape's drop ladder (its foot 1.85 m up):
   - **E pull the ladder down**: my right hand goes up to its lowest rung, closes, and brings it down with it. It lets go and the ladder drops the rest under its own weight, with a bounce and `ladderDrop` (slide, clatter, ring).
   - **W held at its foot climbs** (hold 0.12 s). This is the `climb` controller mode: W up, S down, no momentum. My two hands take the rungs in turn, each solved onto its rung every step. The eye goes over onto the landing: about 3 s up.
   - On the landing, under the rain over the alley, the eye eases along to where the shooter stood. Two brass casings lie there. A torn half of the Cardinal matchbook's back hangs over the landing's outer edge (crew red, half the black bird).
   - **E take**: I go down on my heels, the fingers pinch it up, and it is held up and turned (inspect, like scene 2's matchbook). E puts it away.
   - Back down the ladder on its own, then walking.
5. **halves** (puzzle B). At the doorway's dry stone step under the lamp, **E the matchbook**. The table view looks down on the step.
   - Mack's book lies face down, its back torn across the bird. The torn half lies face up, turned away.

6. **note** (puzzle C). The same step. Seven wet pieces, torn in fixed ragged lines, lie scattered and turned.
   - LMB slides a piece (the topmost under the fingertip comes to the top). The wheel or RMB turns the piece or group under the pointer.
