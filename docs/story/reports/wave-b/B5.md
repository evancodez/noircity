## What plays, beat by beat

**start.** The chapter card (VII The Tail) over the cabin of my coupe at the
kerb: the long hood and its bird ornament, the split screen with the rain on
it and the two wipers sweeping it clear, the dials seen through the wheel,
both gloved hands on the rim. His red tail lamps are small, 70 m down the
river road, the el's arches on my left, the railing and the river and the
city on my right. The engine catches; one prompt: **W drive**.

**chase** (s 14 to 1330 of a 1.76 km road). W / S / A / D, arcade: quick off
the line (0 to 60 km/h in about 2.4 s, 95 to 100 flat out), the lens widens
with speed (68 to 77 degrees), the road's grain and the wheel shiver at speed,
the tail steps out a little when thrown about and comes back, never a spin.
The wheel turns with the steering and the hands ride its rim (object first:
the grips are read off the wheel each step). A wall is a glancing scrape
(sparks, lost speed, the nose turned along it); a column or a post head-on
fast is a hard stop, a thump and a bounce, rolling again at once; a car on a
scripted path (the truck, the car coming the other way) is a moving wall.
He keeps 46 to 26 m ahead on a rubber band (closer as it goes; far ahead he
eases off, so a crash costs ground, not the tail). Through his rear window,
a bowler and a pair of shoulders against his own headlamps. On the way: the
train over the el; board hoardings between the el's columns; the market
(stalls under the el lit by strings of bare bulbs and lanterns, crates and
barrels at the kerb and in the parking lane): he weaves at the stalls and
their crates come into my road; a truck noses out of a side street across a
lane and a half; he clips the newsstand on its corner (a cloud of papers);
headlamps and a horn the other way. Everything that bursts (crates, fruit,
barrels, stalls and their awnings, fences, the newsstand, hydrants with a
geyser) goes up over the hood at the car's speed, falls, bounces and stays;
what lies in the road is kicked again; what flies back into the windscreen
thumps it and may star it. Both cars throw pools of light on the wet road; his
black shape against his own pool is the image of the chase. Failing: he gets
90 m away for 5 s, or I put the car into the truck: black, back to the last
of four marks (s 14, 420, 820, 1200), about 5 s lost.

**close.** Past the mark his top speed drops. Within 30 m, once: **RMB aim,
LMB fire**. RMB: my left hand leaves the wheel, goes down to the holster and
brings the revolver up and out of the window (0.22 s), my head leans out
after it, the mouse aims (the sights sit under the view's centre), A / D
still steer. LMB fires (6 rounds, 0.3 s apart, 1.3 s to reload an empty
cylinder); LMB alone, gun down, snaps it up and fires once the arm is up. A
round into his car cracks and then shatters its glass, breaks a lens, sparks
off the body, takes the tail lamp on that side. His rear tyre goes to a round
near a rear wheel (generous), or to the fifth good round; near the road's end
it goes anyway (mercy for a first-timer who cannot hit it).

**shot.** The tyre bursts (a bang, the rubber flapping), the moment runs slow
for a breath (0.8 s at half speed), my eyes follow him and my car starts to
ease itself to a stop behind where he will end (braking harder and steering
are still mine). He fishtails, comes round a little over half a turn in his
own tyre smoke, and slides side-on up the kerb into a lamp post.

**crash.** The post bends over his car, its lamp swinging and still lit, the
horn stuck and dying, steam off the hood, one tail lamp still red. I stop
9.5 m behind him. **E get out**: the door, out and a step past my wing (my
car is then a wall to a man on foot). He crawls out of his door on hands and
knees and gets up unsteady (`tail_crawl`).

**interrogate.** I walk to him; **E take him**: both gloved fists on his
lapels (the grips are read off his chest every step), him pinned against the
end of his car under the bent post, a thud and a kick of the view
(`tail_pinned`). The one question is asked with a look: the river lies past
the rail at that end of his car, to his side; as my eyes go to the water (a
look at his face does not count) I walk him backward along his car to the
rail and bend him back over it (`tail_overRail`: back far enough that the
water is under his shoulders, his face still up on me, the river and the far
bank's towers behind him). If I only stare at him, after 5.5 s: *MOUSE look
at the river*. He breaks: **"Pier nine."** I let go and step back; he slides
down the railing to the wet pavement (`tail_slide`). The music goes out,
`ctx.next()`.

## Open (most felt first)

1. **The driving is tuned by a bot, not by a hand.** Grip, the slide, the
   steering rate, the rubber band's gap and the scrape all came from scripted
   runs and probes. Nobody has held A and D through the market yet: it may
   feel too sticky or too loose, and the band may feel like a leash.
2. **The sounds are unheard.** Every run was muted, as the rules say. The 132
   sounds render and are not silent offline, but the engine's pitch against
   speed, the tyres, the wipers' thump, the hits, the el's roar, the horn and
   the burst have never been balanced by ear.
3. **The gun out of the window reads small and dark.** The arm and the snub
   revolver are a black mass low in the frame against a dark road; the flash
   splat is big. It works (the play hits the tyre by aiming with the mouse),
   but it does not yet look like a man leaning out to shoot. The revolver is a
   stand-in snub .38 unless a `revolver` prop kind is present; the grip is
   honoured either way.
4. **The hands on the wheel.** Dark gloves on a dark rim read only where the
   windscreen is behind them. There is no hand over hand on a big turn: the
   grips slide round the rim.
5. **The question.** The walk to the rail turns the view with him, since my
   eye rides in front of him. His face is a black mass under the bowler with
   no features. The man is a stand-in: `couple_him`, a bowler. B4's `watcher`
   kind is used by itself when its file is present. The bend over the rail
   is a pose, not a struggle, and no hat goes into the river.
6. **My coupe seen from outside looks lifted off the wet road.** Its
   headlamps' reflections run bright right under it. The contact shadow I
   gave it (the block sedans have one) is drawn but does not darken that
   enough (`09_the_lapels.jpg`).
7. **The spin reads small.** It happens 25 to 40 m ahead through the screen.
   The slow motion and the eyes following him help, but it is a small car
   turning, not a wreck filling the frame (`strip3`).
8. **The river is the road's own wet-stone shader** laid at the water's level.
   It reads as black wet ground more than moving water, and it takes no
   reflections of the far bank's lights.
9. **The headlamp pools are a flat additive wash** on the road, not light.
   They slide over kerbs, crates and his car without being blocked.
10. **The market at 80 to 100 km/h is busy and dark.** The crates are blobs
    until they burst; the bulbs carry it.
11. **The train over the el is hard to see from the driver's seat.** The roof
    header hides the deck. It reads by its rumble, the lamps trembling and
    the shake.
12. **Paths only a human takes.** The bot hits the tyre with its first round,
    so the rear window starring and the fifth-good-round rule have been
    exercised by probes only. The same goes for the mercy tyre and the fail
    and respawn (probe-verified, not in the play).
13. **The start is on this stage, not at B4's alley mouth.** Scene 6 ends in
    another lane's alley; the tail opens on the river road at the kerb, so
    there is a cut between the two chapters.
14. The freighter's hull at the pier is plain. The far bank is flat
    silhouettes.
15. **Merge.** `tools/play.mjs` (the drive step), `tools/plays/slice.json`
