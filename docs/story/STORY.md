# NOIR EXTREME — the story, as built (wave C)

A short noir detective story told by action, objects and staging. One night,
one city, rain all through it. Very little is said: a handful of short lines
as one-line subtitles, never a dialogue tree, never a choice, no voices. If a
beat can be told by an object in the hand, it is. The plan this was built to
is `docs/story/WAVE_C.md` §4 (the story) and §6 (the chapters); what each
chapter does beat by beat is in its lane's report
(`docs/story/reports/wave-c/C<n>.md`); the keys are in the README; the
machinery is `ARCHITECTURE.md` §11. (Wave B's longer bible, with The Body
and the river road, is in git history: `git show 03728e5:docs/story/STORY.md`.)

## The rules of the telling

- **Year** 1937. The city has no name.
- **The detective** (the player): never seen, never named. Only his hands and
  sleeves. First person always.
- **The crew's colour is red.** The Cardinal is the man whose colour is red;
  red means his crew every time it shows: the matchbook, the crosses on
  Ames's card and on Dot's photograph, his tail lamps, the neon of his club.
- **Two red crosses are the story.** The first (on Ames's card) I read right.
  The second (on Dot's photograph) shows me I read the check wrong.
- **Faces are never modelled.** Every face is lost under a brim, a cap, hair,
  or the light behind. People are read by silhouette and one prop.
- **Colour** is an accent, one or two per frame: blood, fire, an ember,
  scotch, neon, the crew's red.
- **Text in the world** is short: a sign, a name on a door, three numbers.

## The night, chapter by chapter

**The opening stills** (BEGIN only): a receiver in a hand under a desk lamp,
`A TIP. AMES.`; three hats in a doorway's light round a small white apron,
`HIS MEN. MY DINER.`; the diner's neon through a wet windscreen.

**I The Diner** (`diner`). On a stool at the counter: light up, order a
scotch from Dot, drink, watch the big man two stools down who has watched
her all night, pay. Mack: `She's got nothing to say to you.` Nothing to lose
here; it is the room and the people.

**II The Fight** (`fight`, no cut from I). Mack's hand on my shoulder, the
stool goes, fists up. A short lesson (jab with either button, block with
SPACE, the dodge with A / D on the cue), then three rounds: his tells, a
slow-down and a cue on the side to slip to for every big swing and every
thing he throws, openings to punish with a held punch. He goes out through
the window. The street door is barred during the fight; I lift the bar, push
it, and find on him the red matchbook and **Ames's card, crossed through in
red**. Still: `AMES. MARKED.`

**III Room 304** (`office`). The Harbor Building's third floor; Ames is dead
at his desk: too late. A real puzzle: the pad on his blotter holds the
impression of what he wrote last - find a pencil, shade the pad and the
safe's three numbers come up big and white; the safe is behind a picture,
its dial turned by the mouse. In the safe,
a torn guest check from my diner, pieced together on the desk (drag a piece,
turn it): `PIER 9 · MIDNIGHT · — DOT`, in a woman's hand. Stills: `DOT'S
HAND.` / `SHE SENT HIM TO THEM.` I come to think she is with the Cardinal.

**IV The Door** (`gunfight`, the same room, no black). The revolver out
(six and a pocket). The door swings shut; their shadows on the frosted glass;
a moment to choose: tip Ames's desk and get behind it, or fire through the
glass first. Then they come, and not all through one door: the room, a man
round by the next office, the corridor when I check it, the heavy last.
Quiet: `See if more are coming.` At the street window, a man under a lamp
under an umbrella, looking up.

**V The Watcher** (`watcher`, the window's frame is both chapters). He runs.
Out of the fire-escape window, down the iron, and after him on foot through
the backlot, the alleys, a press hall, the kitchens and the roofs to the
avenue under the el and the market. Nothing goes over anything by itself:
SPACE over, C under, SPACE through a door, a fork with two ways. I can lose
him on several misses or if he gets too far ahead (`He's getting away.`
first). He reaches his car; mine is at the kerb: straight into the seat, no
fade.

**VI The Tail** (`tail`). A city, not a road: turns, traffic, a river road.
I tail his red tail lamps; too close and he speeds up, too far for too long
and he is gone (warned first). When he makes for the docks the revolver comes
out of the window: my rounds hurt his car, a shunt on his back corner spins
it. He is crouched by the wreck and says `Pier 9.` I drive to Pier 9, a place
on this map, and stop at its gate.

**VII Pier Nine** (`warehouse`). Empty gun, so quiet: across the dark shed to
the Cardinal's lit office on the mezzanine, out of the light, low (C), with
bottles to throw, a switch, a lantern and a load to drop to draw men away,
and blows from behind. Under the office window: `The waitress talked.`
`The diner. Tonight.` `No one walks out.` In his office, **Dot's photograph,
crossed through in red**: the same cross as Ames's card. Still: `NOT HIS
GIRL. HIS NEXT.` I had it backwards: she was Ames's witness, and she is
next. Shells in his drawer: the telephone rings, and out loud: the gunmen
on the floor, the catwalk, to the loading bay. The foreman bars the way, his
hook takes the revolver into the river: fists. He goes through the planks
into the water. To the car.

**VIII The Block** (`shootout`). Still: `THE DINER.` In the nick of time:
through Mack's broken window Sal has his gun on Dot at the back bar, Nicky
watches the street at the door. Three ways in (from behind Nicky and the
door, a bottle into the road, the broken window and low up the counter's
aisle) before Sal's clock runs out. Then Dot reaches under the counter and
lays the Tommy gun in front of me: she is the one who arms me (`Ames said
you'd come.`). The street: two cars, men from the el's columns, the alley's
corner and the kitchen's hatch, a man on the el, a drive-by that comes round
twice; drums are few (Dot keeps one a movement). His armoured saloon comes
up the avenue for the door: the lamps, then the tyres. The Cardinal on foot
behind his shield man, open only after his own shot, his man's fall, a car
going up or the hydrant. Three honest hits, then ten seconds to finish it
before he reaches Dot.

**The end.** His hat in the wet street, the cigar's ember beside it, Dot at
the broken window, the train going away down the el. Still: the hat, the
ember. THE END, and a click goes to the title.

## What the detective carries

After II the matchbook and Ames's crossed card; after III the check
(`flags.note = 'PIER 9'`); the revolver from IV (it was always under his arm,
the pocket counted); empty after VI; the photograph and the shells from VII
(the hook takes the revolver); the Tommy gun in VIII, from Dot.
